# OBJECTIVE FIELD
### A design philosophy

---

**Objective Field** begins where decoration ends. It holds that a composition earns its
authority by structure alone — that a grid, held without deviation across every element,
communicates more conviction than any flourish laid on top. The work must read as though
a module was chosen first and everything after it obeyed: each measure a multiple, each
interval deliberate, each edge answering another edge somewhere else on the page. This is
slow work. It should look slow — the product of deep expertise, of someone who has drawn
the same layout twenty times and kept the twentieth.

**Space and form.** Asymmetry is the governing instinct. The page is divided into unequal
fields, and the tension between a dense block and a wide emptiness carries the composition.
Nothing is centred out of habit; a thing sits where the grid puts it, and the grid is never
sentimental. Every element hangs from a single left edge — a spine the eye learns within a
second and then trusts completely — while the right side is left ragged and open. Void is
not leftover room. It is the largest shape on the page, and it is sized with the same
painstaking attention as the type.

**Colour and material.** Two values do the work: a near-black and the ground it sits on.
The ground is off-white and faintly warm, because a printed sheet is never the white of a
screen. Against those two, exactly one chromatic accent is admitted — saturated, unmixed,
and used so sparingly that its every appearance reads as a decision. It may mark, divide,
or count; it may never fill or decorate. Where texture enters, it enters below the
threshold of notice: the tooth of paper, nothing more.

**Scale and rhythm.** Hierarchy is built from few sizes separated by large intervals. A
display size that dominates without apology; a reading size; a marking size for the small
systematic labels that index the work. Between them, no timid gradations — the jump itself
is the statement. Repetition supplies the counter-rhythm: rows at identical heights, rules
at identical weights, a field of accumulated marks whose regularity rewards a second and
third look. The eye should be able to measure the page and find it exact.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Rules are structure, never garnish: a hairline separates, a
heavy bar concludes, and both align to the module. Imagery, when it appears, is reduced
until it becomes notation — a pattern of marks standing for a thing rather than a drawing
of it, indistinguishable in its geometry from the rest of the system. The result should
feel less designed than *determined*, as though the arrangement were the only solution and
the designer merely found it after countless hours of looking.

**Text as a visual element.** Words are set flush left, ragged right, unjustified and
unhyphenated, in a neutral grotesque that makes no argument of its own. They are few. Each
one is named rather than described — no persuasion, no adjectives, no explanatory block.
Legibility is absolute and non-negotiable: the piece must hold at a quarter of its size, in
bad light, at a glance, with no character crowded and nothing touching an edge it was not
built to touch. Nothing overlaps. Nothing is approximate. Master-level execution here looks
like restraint carried further than is comfortable, and then held.
